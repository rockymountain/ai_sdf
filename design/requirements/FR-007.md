# Intent

Autonomous implementation work must stop after two failed attempts for the same
execution scope without relying on a human to watch the terminal, on process-local
state, or on provider-specific controls.

# Acceptance

`constitution/policies.yaml` is the sole executable authority for the
`autonomous_execution` namespace and its `max_attempts`, initially `2`. Missing,
malformed, unsupported, or unreadable policy fails closed; prompts, provider
configuration, adapter metadata, projected agent instructions, and hardcoded
fallbacks are not policy authority.

`execution_scope_id` is the stable governed execution-objective identity and owns
implementation-attempt budget when attempts apply. Non-implementation invocations
retain the same scope for attribution, provenance, lifecycle, and circuit
enforcement without creating capacity. An attempt is one autonomous implementation
plus deterministic test/validation cycle for that scope. Runtime-invocation success alone does not complete the
attempt. A deterministic acceptance checkpoint marks success or failure.
Review/orchestration usage is charged to the DEV task but does not automatically
reserve another implementation attempt.

`invocation_purpose=implementation` requires attempt capacity.
`invocation_purpose=continuation` requires an existing consumed attempt and P1-I16
lineage. Human-authorized `acceptance_validation`, initially DEV-007's live proof,
and review/orchestration purposes may omit reservation and attempt identity, but
cannot perform autonomous implementation work or create or replenish attempt budget.
Any invocation without implementation-attempt identity is prohibited from
repository/workspace implementation mutation. Mutation requires purpose
`implementation` with reservation identity or `continuation` with an existing
consumed-attempt lineage; purpose labels alone do not authorize capability.

A watchdog timeout for `acceptance_validation`, review, or orchestration creates no
consumed attempt or budget capacity. It stops autonomous follow-on in the scope until
governed human disposition; any later non-mutating action remains explicitly
authorized and outside implementation-attempt accounting.

Every attempt-slot reservation has a unique SDF-owned `reservation_id` and a
`candidate_attempt_number`. Attempt
reservation, remaining-budget evaluation, reservation-state transitions,
consumed-attempt binding, and the `CIRCUIT_OPEN` transition are atomic for one scope.
`RESERVED` exclusively holds a `candidate_attempt_number` so concurrent processes
cannot reserve the same active slot. Reservation alone does not create an
`attempt_number` or consume budget.

Affirmative evidence that the runtime or provider accepted execution transitions
the reservation to `CONSUMED` and binds its `candidate_attempt_number` as
`attempt_number`. Affirmative evidence that runtime execution definitely did not
start transitions it to `RELEASED`. Insufficient evidence to establish either
outcome transitions it to `UNRESOLVED`.
Consumed attempts are unique by `execution_scope_id + attempt_number`.

A proven local pre-invocation failure before runtime start transitions the
reservation to `RELEASED` without consuming budget. Its reservation record and
failure evidence remain immutable, but it no longer occupies the candidate slot. A
later reservation for the same scope may claim that same next candidate number with
a new `reservation_id`. Released evidence must not be deleted or overwritten to
reuse the slot.

If evidence is insufficient to prove either accepted execution or definite
non-start, the reservation remains unavailable in `UNRESOLVED`, autonomous
continuation stops, and governed human resolution is
required; it is never released automatically. Later affirmative non-start evidence
may resolve it to `RELEASED`; later affirmative accepted-execution evidence may
resolve it to `CONSUMED`; insufficient evidence leaves it `UNRESOLVED`. Every runtime
adapter defines its concrete evidence mapping in conformance specifications. The
deterministic acceptance
checkpoint closes a `CONSUMED` attempt as success or failure.

If evidence remains permanently insufficient, the old reservation stays immutable
and `UNRESOLVED`, its scope stays stopped, and budget is not released or restored.
Further work requires a human-authorized successor `execution_scope_id` recording
`predecessor_scope_id`, actor, timestamp, reason, and disposition. A fresh unlinked
scope must not bypass the unresolved reservation.

For one `execution_scope_id`, at most one implementation attempt may be open. A new
reservation is rejected while the scope has an active `RESERVED` reservation, an
`UNRESOLVED` reservation, or a consumed attempt not yet closed by deterministic
acceptance. This rule is durable across restart and atomic across concurrent
processes. A passed attempt accepts the scope and permits no next attempt. Only a
failed closed attempt with remaining budget permits the next reservation.

The consumed-attempt lifecycle is separate from reservation state:
`OPEN -> PASSED|FAILED`, with `OPEN -> SUSPENDED -> OPEN` allowed only for a governed
continuation and uncertain non-resumable terminal evidence entering
`RECONCILIATION_REQUIRED`. In Phase 1.0, only affirmative evidence of
`terminal_status=interrupted` and `terminal_reason=usage_limit` permits suspension
and possible resume; every resume requires recorded human authorization,
and the implementation objective, scope, predecessor lineage, and consumed attempt
remain unchanged.
Watchdog timeout, runtime failure, network error, cancellation, and unknown terminal
state are not automatically resumable. They require reconciliation and cannot be
relabeled to avoid attempt accounting. A continuation creates a new `invocation_id`
and does not create or replenish an implementation attempt.

From `RECONCILIATION_REQUIRED`, affirmative evidence of
`terminal_status=interrupted` and `terminal_reason=usage_limit` moves the attempt to
`SUSPENDED`, after which a human may authorize P1-I16 continuation. Recovered
deterministic checkpoint evidence closes it as `PASSED` or `FAILED` according to the
checkpoint. Otherwise, record human disposition, keep the scope stopped, and require
a human-authorized successor scope for further work. Human resolution cannot
manufacture deterministic acceptance, free consumed budget, or create a retry.

After two consumed attempts fail, the runtime persists the second failure and open
circuit before any invocation for a new implementation attempt can start through
`AIRuntimePort`. No candidate attempt 3 may become consumed. Continuations within an
already-consumed open attempt do not change `max_attempts` accounting, so raw
invocation count is never used as an attempt-count proxy. New SDF `run_id` or
`invocation_id` values do not replenish scope budget. Optional runtime or
provider-native IDs cannot affect budget identity.

After `CIRCUIT_OPEN`, the existing scope remains closed. Further work requires a
human-authorized successor `execution_scope_id` recording predecessor scope, actor,
timestamp, reason, and disposition. The old scope, attempts, reservations,
invocation evidence, and usage remain immutable. Phase 1.0 provides no same-scope
budget reset.
